# Summary

ProteinToolkit is a Python package that takes a protein from raw sequence to
a confidence-scored functional report. A sequence-only front end computes
composition statistics, scans motifs and catalytic-residue patterns, and
predicts the top-level Enzyme Commission (EC) class with a gradient-boosted
classifier. If a structure is available (a local file or an RCSB PDB
identifier), the same report is extended with pocket detection, a spatial
check of catalytic-residue geometry, and AutoDock Vina docking
[@trott2010vina; @eberhardt2021vina] of a query ligand or a whole ligand
library, whose results are fused with the sequence-level evidence. Each
component reports its limitations alongside its output, and the classifier's
confidence is checked for calibration rather than assumed.

# Statement of need

Tools for enzyme function annotation typically specialise: sequence
classifiers, pocket detectors, or docking engines. The closest integrated
tool is CAPIM [@ozsari2025capim], which combines pocket detection, catalytic-site annotation and docking for
multimeric proteins; its structural methods are more rigorous than
those here. ProteinToolkit's contribution is different: a cheap,
structure-free triage stage so that many sequences can be screened and only
the promising ones sent to the expensive structural stage, and a validation
discipline that is built into the repository (below) rather than described
once in prose. It is aimed at researchers who want a transparent, scriptable
first-pass pipeline, not at users who need state-of-the-art accuracy in any
single stage.

# Functionality

- **Sequence triage** (`main.py`, `triage.py`): composition and physico-chemical
  features, motif and catalytic-residue rules, EC-class prediction (XGBoost
  on 430 sequence-derived features, trained on UniProt [@uniprot2023]
  reviewed entries; or, opt-in, a classifier on frozen ESM-1b embeddings
  [@rives2021esm]), batch ranking of FASTA files.
- **Structure** (`structure/`): PDB parsing and fetching, contact-based pocket
  scoring, and a centroid-distance check that candidate catalytic residues
  are close together in 3D.
- **Docking and screening** (`docking/`, `screen.py`, `pipeline.py`): receptor
  protonation at pH 7.4 (PDB2PQR [@dolinsky2004pdb2pqr] with PROPKA
  [@olsson2011propka]) and AutoDock typing and charging (Meeko), ligand
  preparation (RDKit, Meeko), Vina docking in a box sized to the
  ligand, pose analysis (contacts, hydrogen bonds, metal coordination,
  clashes) feeding a fitted estimate of the probability that a pose is within
  2 Å of the true binding pose, banded as STRONG/MODERATE/WEAK, and library
  screening that prepares the receptor and pockets once.
- **Reliability reporting** (`ml/novelty.py`, `core/reliability.py`,
  `ml/selective_gate.py`): each EC prediction carries its similarity to the
  nearest training family, a novelty tier and a recalibrated confidence;
  docking and screening outputs quote the accuracy the committed benchmarks
  measured for them; and an optional triage gate chooses its cutoff from
  cluster-aware calibration data.

# Validation

All numbers below are regenerated by scripts under `benchmarks/` and `ml/`
and the raw outputs are committed to the repository.

**Redocking.** Four crystal complexes (3HS4, 3PTB, 1STP, 181L) were
redocked into their own proteins with three random seeds. The top-ranked pose
was within 2 Å of the crystal pose in 9 of 12 runs (best of nine poses: 12 of
12). The only systematic failure is acetazolamide in carbonic anhydrase
(3HS4): Vina's scoring has no explicit treatment of zinc-coordinating
sulfonamides, and its top-ranked pose is 5.3 Å from the crystal pose in every
seed (a pose within 1.4--1.8 Å exists but is not ranked first). Running this
benchmark caught a receptor-preparation bug that had produced 0 of 4
successes (the prepared receptor still contained the crystal ligand and
crystallisation additives) and an inconsistency between Vina's score table
and its output file when poses fall outside its energy window. Four
complexes is a correctness check, not a docking benchmark; comparison on
CASF-2016 [@su2019casf] against a second engine is future work. These
numbers were produced twice: first with a receptor that had no hydrogens
and zero partial charges, then with the receptor protonated at pH 7.4 and
typed with AutoDock donor atoms. The success count did not change (9 of
12 both times, with the same three complexes succeeding in every seed);
best-of-nine improved from 11 to 12 of 12 and the top-1 acetazolamide error
grew from 3.3 to 5.3 Å. We read the persistent acetazolamide miss as a
missing metal-coordination term, which receptor hydrogens do not supply;
we did not test that.

The built-in site finder fails this test. The pocket detector builds pockets
from the structure's HETATM records, so on a holo structure it simply
reports the ligand site. With every HETATM removed so that cavity detection
must work from protein geometry alone, the selected pocket was 6--11 Å from
the true site in all four complexes. One of the four top-ranked poses (3HS4,
1.3 Å) was within 2 Å, but only because the padded docking box around the
wrong pocket still enclosed 12 of the crystal ligand's 13 heavy atoms; it is
not evidence of site finding. The toolkit should therefore be used with a
known site.

**Screening enrichment.** For two targets, carbonic anhydrase II (a zinc
metalloenzyme) and estrogen receptor α (no metal), 50 ChEMBL actives
[@zdrazil2024chembl] (binding assays, pChEMBL ≥ 7) were docked into the known
site together with 50 measured weak binders of the same target (pChEMBL ≤ 5)
matched to each active's heavy-atom count. The best Vina score separated
them no better than chance within the uncertainty: ROC AUC 0.49 (bootstrap
95% CI 0.38--0.61) for carbonic anhydrase II and 0.60 (0.49--0.70) for
estrogen receptor α; top-10% enrichment factors were 0.7 and 1.6 (random is
1.0), and scores did not correlate with potency among the actives (Spearman
0.03 and 0.04). These are the results with the receptor protonated at pH 7.4
and charged; with the earlier hydrogen-free, zero-charge receptor the AUCs
were 0.58 (0.47--0.69) and 0.58 (0.46--0.68), so adding receptor hydrogens
changed the per-ligand scores (they became about 0.2--0.4 kcal/mol more
favourable on average, and their rank order stayed correlated with the
earlier one at Spearman 0.9) but not the conclusion. A
heavy-atom-count control scores 0.50 by construction. On carbonic anhydrase
II, 86% of actives but 6% of weak binders carry a primary sulfonamide (the
zinc-binding group), and that substructure alone reaches AUC 0.90, so a
one-line rule outperforms the docking score. We report this as a negative
result: in this pipeline, Vina is a way to generate plausible poses in a
known site, not to rank compounds by potency; the pose verdict estimates
whether a pose is geometrically right (next paragraph), not whether the
compound binds. Both sets are small, so intervals are wide, and measured weak binders are a
harder negative class than property-matched decoys [@mysinger2012dude].

**Pose plausibility.** A pose's STRONG/MODERATE/WEAK label is only useful if
it tracks whether the pose is right, which redocking lets us check. We
selected 38 crystal complexes from RCSB by rule (X-ray, at most 2.0 Å, one
protein chain, one drug-like ligand of 8--28 heavy atoms, fixed random seed),
docked each ligand back into its own site and measured every pose's RMSD to
the crystal pose; 30 complexes docked (243 poses, 13% within 2 Å, and Vina's
first pose right for 15 of 30). Our original rule-based label separated right
from wrong poses poorly (AUC 0.63--0.70; a chemically motivated change that
counted aromatic carbons as hydrophobic lowered it from 0.70 to 0.63).
Replacing it with a logistic model of pose correctness over the score, its
gap to the best pose, and contact counts gives an AUC of 0.82 (bootstrap 95%
interval 0.73--0.90) under leave-one-complex-out cross-validation, a gain of
0.19 over the rule-based label with the aromatic change (interval 0.09--0.28;
0.12 over the original rule) that cleared a threshold we fixed beforehand
(0.05). On four complexes the model never saw (35 poses)
its AUC was 0.85, against 0.61 for the Vina score and 0.43 for the rule-based
label.
For each complex's top pose, the observed hit rates were 21%, 57% and 89% for
the WEAK, MODERATE and STRONG bands (14, 7 and 9 complexes), so the
probabilities are usable but rest on few complexes, and very small ligands
are still rated low when correct (benzene: a correct pose rated 14%).

**Catalytic-system rules.** The sequence-only rule layer originally reported a
serine, cysteine or aspartic protease or a metalloprotease whenever the protein
contained enough histidine, serine, aspartate or similar residues anywhere,
which nearly every protein does. Scored against UniProt EC subclasses, that
rule fired on every non-peptidase tested (about four systems each) and was
right 13--15% of the time; it also drove a "zinc metalloprotease" function and
a Zn²⁺ ligand call for every protein, serine proteases included. Requiring a
conserved active-site motif for each system, with patterns taken from PROSITE
and screened against labelled sequences, raises precision to 90--97% on a
held-out draw of 2,000 sequences disjoint from those used to select the
motifs, with recall of 35% (serine), 39% (cysteine), 45% (aspartic) and 89%
(metallo); 2% of non-peptidases receive any hit, and the zinc claim falls from
100% to 0% for serine and cysteine proteases and 2--9% for other non-zinc
proteins while remaining at 89% for true metalloproteases. Recall is partial
by construction, so the absence of a hit is not evidence against a protease.
(The validation also caught an error in one of these patterns, written from
memory with the wrong residue spacing: correcting it raised held-out recall for
cysteine proteases from 31% to 39% and precision from 91% to 97%.)

**Catalytic-site identification.** A structure-based detector that looks for the
catalytic residues of these four protease systems in 3D originally accepted any
residues of the right types lying near each other. On 15 classic proteases
with textbook catalytic residues it named the right system but often the wrong
residues (the serine nucleophile was wrong in trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase
and thrombin; both pepsin aspartates were wrong) and added false extra systems
to every structure. Requiring the conserved sequence motif, taking the
residues that motif marks, measuring between side-chain functional atoms and
linking residues as a connected cluster within 5 Å, and reading modified
residues through `MODRES`, recovers 17 of 17 catalytic residues on the seven
structures used to design it and 16 of 18 on eight held-out structures, with no
false systems on any of the 15. Two caveats limit this. The held-out set is
small and was written down after the design, one structure (actinidin) led to
the modified-residue fix and is therefore partly development, and the
endothiapepsin truth was corrected from literature numbering to the file's own
after inspection; and caspase-3 is missed because its catalytic cysteine and
histidine are 5.95 Å apart in that crystal. The detector marks a plausible
catalytic cluster for four protease systems only; it is not a general
active-site predictor.

**Leakage-aware EC classification.** Randomly splitting UniProt sequences
lets near-duplicates land in both training and test sets. Sequences were
grouped by 5-mer containment (about 55% identity over an end-to-end
alignment), and whole groups were held out. Under the original random split
the classifier reported 85.0% accuracy, but 86.6% of test sequences had a
grouped training neighbour; accuracy was 90.4% on those and 50.3% on test
sequences without one. With cluster-aware splitting the model reaches
66.0% held-out accuracy over six EC classes (grouped five-fold CV 62.8% ±
2.0%; 15,725 test sequences), with expected calibration error 0.045 and
mild overconfidence. The grouping is a k-mer proxy for CD-HIT
[@li2006cdhit] or MMseqs2 [@steinegger2017mmseqs2] and will not catch remote
homologs, so 66% is still likely optimistic for truly novel folds.

**An optional protein-language-model classifier.** Replacing hand-built
sequence features by mean ESM-1b embeddings [@rives2021esm] and training a
one-hidden-layer classifier on them (same cluster-aware split, model and
early-stopping epoch chosen on a cluster-grouped validation split inside the
training data, one temperature fitted there, test set used once) raises
held-out accuracy from 66% to 92.4%, and to 94.2% in a second run trained on
less data, so the two runs differ by about two points of single-seed
variance. Mean confidence is 0.933 and expected calibration error 0.0096
(0.043 before temperature scaling), so the higher confidence is earned: 81% of
sequences receive confidence of at least 0.9 and are right 98.8% of the time.
By 5-mer similarity to the training families the accuracy is 82.7%, 93.6% and
98.3% for novel, related and familiar sequences. Two caveats bound these
numbers. The language model was pretrained without labels on UniRef50, which
contains relatives of nearly every test sequence, and it recognises remote
homologs that our 5-mer clustering cannot separate; in the fifth of test
sequences least similar to training in embedding space, accuracy is 78% and
confidence runs about 8 points high. And the novelty recalibration that
repairs the feature-based model does not help here (calibration error 0.0096
against 0.0098 out-of-fold), because the embeddings already encode novelty.
The classifier needs the 650M-parameter model at inference, so it is opt-in.

**Novelty-aware confidence.** The held-out sequences still differ in how close
they are to the training data, and accuracy follows that closely. Splitting
them by 5-mer similarity to the nearest training family, the model is right
46% of the time on the most novel fifth, 65% on the middle three fifths and
90% on the most familiar fifth, while its own confidence averages 0.61, 0.69
and 0.85: it is overconfident exactly where the input is unfamiliar. A
three-parameter logistic recalibration on (confidence, log similarity) lowers
expected calibration error from 0.045 to 0.019 overall and from 0.154 to
0.049 on the novel fifth, evaluated out-of-fold with folds grouped by
sequence cluster; the Brier score improves from 0.170 to 0.165 and the ROC
AUC for "this prediction is correct" from 0.804 to 0.813. These gains are
modest in discrimination and real in calibration. The held-out set spans
only a limited similarity range, so the adjustment is unvalidated outside
it, and the similarity measure is a k-mer proxy that misses remote
homology.

# Limitations

Receptor preparation handles a single chain; metals are added after
protonation (PDB2PQR cannot see them), cofactors receive no hydrogens or
charges, and residues without a backbone are omitted. Vina ignores partial
charges, so the charge assignment is inert for the scoring used here.
Vina scores are not calibrated affinities and, as
shown above, do not rank potent against weak binders of one target. The
pose verdict is a modest probability estimate fitted to 30 complexes and
cannot rule out a confidently wrong pose. The confidences of the catalytic-system
rules are measured held-out precisions, but the motif, function and ligand
confidences remain hand-tuned weights, not probabilities. The
repository's README keeps this list current.
